An interface for going somewhere

Less scrolling.
More out there.

A trip-first experience for riders, hikers, road-trippers and curious explorers. Discover a route, prepare with confidence, record the journey and shape the moments into a story — without borrowing another platform’s social feed identity.

Design principle
Adventure is the main event. Community and storytelling support the journey; they do not compete with route planning, field safety cues or personal privacy.

Screen 01 / Basecamp

Home as a launchpad, not an endless feed.

Basecamp asks the rider’s next useful question and gives three clear paths: plan something, start the field session, or craft an existing memory.

Primary hierarchyOne dominant “Build an adventure” action, with field mode and story editing as distinct secondary tasks.
Personal continuityResume a saved trip with progress, pending kit checks and a draft state — not a popularity-driven timeline.
Multi-activityThe same home supports motorcycles, cycling, walking, camping, road trips and water activities.
Information densityCompact operational cards keep the experience useful rather than engagement-driven.
Original pattern: “Basecamp” and “Field Mode” establish the app’s own vocabulary. The outdoor visual language is a broad category cue, not a clone of a specific platform.

Screen 03 / Route brief

Turn a candidate into a plan.

This bridge prevents route discovery from jumping straight into recording. Inspect route chapters, add stops, identify unknowns and prepare before starting field mode.

Route chaptersBreak a journey into scannable stops with notes tied to each waypoint.
Ready-to-go signalShow checklist progress without suggesting completion guarantees safety.
Fallback planningKeep a retreat/alternative route and expected return schedule.
Explicit estimatesLabel distance and duration as estimates until provider routing exists.
Photos and clips can connect to actual trip chapters later, rather than being unrelated posts.

Screen 04 / Field mode

Keep the active session calm and legible.

Field mode has one dominant state, minimal actions, high-contrast metrics and clear disclosure of what data is real. A riding interface should minimise distraction and never imply SOS or location sharing works if it is not connected.

Distinct statesReady → Active → Paused → Finished; labels and controls adapt to state.
Glanceable controlsStart, pause/resume and finish are easy to predict. Deep editing stays outside an active session.
Honest telemetryUnavailable speed, altitude, GPS quality and SOS states must be explicitly unavailable.
Return & check-inShow chosen contact and expected return while distinguishing local reminders from actual delivery.
Timer and distance are simulated in this prototype. No GPS, contact messages or emergency dispatch are connected.

Screen 05 / Story Studio

A creative workspace, not a post composer clone.

Story Studio begins with a trip artifact: format, tone, editable title/caption, chosen audience and explicit location protections. Output can be a journal, recap, route guide or photo essay.

Artifact-ledChoose structured story formats tied to trips and chapters.
User controlChoose tone, language and media before any AI request; keep output editable.
Privacy by defaultRoute masking and delayed location are first-class controls.
Consent boundaryMake upload destination and retention explicit before media leaves the device.
Audience defaults to “Only me.” Production publishing must enforce privacy server-side.

Screen 06 / Share review

Review is a real step, not a decorative dialog.

The preview reflects edits. Audience, precise-route treatment and delayed location are summarised before confirmation; three consent checks keep the final action gated.

Live previewTitle, caption and format flow from the editor.
Plain disclosureTranslate privacy settings into plain language at the point of action.
Blocking validationThe final action is disabled until audience, location and media-rights reviews are acknowledged.
Reversible stepsUsers can return to edit without losing fields; avoid ambiguous gesture-based publishing.
The final action only confirms the local demo. Production requires authenticated backend, storage and privacy enforcement.

Screen 07 / Trail settings

Preferences should shape utility and calm.

A multi-activity explorer should not force one identity or a generic social feed. Settings make relevance, quiet hours, voice behaviour and trip privacy explicit.

Multi-activityChoose several modes and add interests later.
Notification pacingQuiet time and mood are editable and resettable.
Voice transparencyShow exactly when the microphone or listener is active.
Privacy controlsExpose exact coordinates, history, export/delete and story audiences in plain language.
The settings page is utility-oriented. Follower counts and engagement streaks are not needed for the core product.
